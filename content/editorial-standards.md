# How Solar Planner Guide Is Written

Solar Planner Guide helps homeowners decide whether solar makes sense for them: costs, incentives, financing, contracts and choosing an installer. This page explains, plainly, how the articles are made.

## How articles are made

Articles on Solar Planner Guide are drafted with AI writing tools. Each draft starts from public reference sources on the topic, including those listed below, and from recent news when a topic is changing.

Before an article is published, a second automated pass reviews it and removes or rewrites anything it cannot stand behind: statistics, prices or dates it is not confident are correct, studies or organizations that may be misattributed, and personal stories presented as if they happened to the writer.

Articles are not reviewed by a licensed installer or financial advisor before publication. Use them as background for a conversation with one, not as a substitute.

The site owner audits published articles in batches, merging pages that overlap, deleting weak ones and correcting errors.

## Who writes here

The bylines on Solar Planner Guide (Alex Rivera, Craig Stevens, Derek Hansen, Nadia Patel, Patricia Moore) are pen names, one for each area of coverage. They are not real individuals, they hold no professional licenses, and their profile images are illustrations, not photographs of real people.

## Sources we rely on

- [U.S. Dept. of Energy: Solar](https://www.energy.gov/eere/solar/homeowners-guide-going-solar): Homeowner solar guidance
- [National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL)](https://www.nrel.gov/): Solar research and data
- [DSIRE: Incentives Database](https://www.dsireusa.org/): State solar incentives
- [EPA: Green Power](https://www.epa.gov/greenpower): Clean-energy information

Where an article depends on a specific figure, rule or study, it links to or names the source so you can check it.

## Limits of this site

Incentives, net-metering rules and utility rates change, sometimes mid-year, and they differ by state and utility. Our savings figures and calculators are estimates. Get written quotes from more than one licensed installer, read any loan, lease or power purchase agreement in full, and confirm incentives with your utility and state energy office before you sign.

## Corrections

If you find an error, please report it through the [contact page](/contact/). Reports go directly to the site owner, and confirmed errors are corrected in the article.

## How the site makes money

Solar Planner Guide may earn money from display advertising and from affiliate links, including Amazon links (as an Amazon Associate, the site earns from qualifying purchases). Advertisers and affiliate partners do not see or approve articles before they are published.
